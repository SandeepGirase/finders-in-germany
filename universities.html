01 — Universities

Finding the right university, and getting admitted

Germany has more than 400 state-recognised institutions and three different types of higher-education degree. Here's how to narrow the field, check whether your existing qualification is accepted, and apply before the deadline.

02 — Degree types

Universität, Fachhochschule, or duale Hochschule?

The type of institution shapes how academic or how practical your programme will be — and it affects admission requirements.

TypeFocusGood fit for
Universität (research university)Theory-led, research-orientedStudents aiming for academic research or a PhD
Fachhochschule / University of Applied SciencesPractical, industry-linked, includes internshipsStudents who want hands-on training and faster entry to the job market
Duale Hochschule (dual study)Alternates classroom study with paid work at a partner companyStudents who want to earn while they study and secure early industry contacts

03 — Application steps

The application process, in order

Before you apply

Shortlist programmes on Hochschulkompass

Germany's official database of accredited degree programmes lets you filter by subject, city, language of instruction and degree type. Shortlist 5–10 programmes with a realistic mix of competitive and safer options.

4–8 weeks

Check whether your qualification is recognised

If your school-leaving certificate is from outside the EU, you may need an APS Certificate (Academic Evaluation Centre) before a university will even review your application. Check the requirement for your home country early — it's often the slowest step.

Ongoing

Prove your language proficiency

German-taught programmes usually require TestDaF or DSH; English-taught programmes usually require IELTS or TOEFL. Book your test date months ahead — slots fill up around application deadlines.

Deadline-driven

Apply through uni-assist or directly to the university

Many universities centralise international applications through uni-assist; others accept direct applications. Confirm which route each shortlisted programme uses — submitting to the wrong one wastes your fee and your time.

After admission

Accept your offer and request your admission letter

Your Zulassungsbescheid (admission letter) is the document that unlocks your visa application, so request it in a format your embassy accepts, and keep both digital and printed copies.

04 — Deadlines

Two intakes a year

Most programmes admit students twice a year. Missing a deadline usually means waiting a full six months, so build your timeline backwards from these dates.

SemesterTypical deadlineClasses begin
Winter semester15 JulyEarly October
Summer semester15 JanuaryEarly April

Deadlines vary by university and by whether you apply through uni-assist — always confirm the exact date on the programme page.

05 — Funding

Scholarships worth checking

Most public universities charge no tuition, but living costs still need to be covered. The DAAD (German Academic Exchange Service) is the largest source of scholarships for international students, alongside university-specific and foundation scholarships.

Before you rely on a scholarship

Apply for the blocked account (see Pre-Arrival) assuming you won't win a scholarship. It's far easier to cancel a backup plan than to scramble for funds after a visa appointment.
