02 — Pre-Arrival

Everything to arrange before you fly

Once you have your admission letter, a handful of tasks stand between you and your flight — most of them slow-moving, government-run, and unforgiving of missing paperwork. Start these as early as possible.

First

Apply for your national (D) student visa

Book an appointment at the German embassy or consulate in your home country as soon as you have an admission letter — appointment slots are often the real bottleneck, not the paperwork itself. Bring your passport, admission letter, proof of funds, health insurance, and biometric photos.

2–3 weeks

Open a blocked account (Sperrkonto)

Germany requires proof you can support yourself: a blocked account that releases a fixed monthly amount. The required minimum balance is set by the government and adjusted periodically, so confirm the current figure before you transfer funds — providers such as Fintiba and Expatrio are commonly used by international students.

Before your visa appointment

Arrange health insurance

You'll need incoming travel insurance to apply for your visa, then switch to German public health insurance (gesetzliche Krankenversicherung) once you arrive — most public universities require it for enrolment. Students over 30 or in some programmes may need private insurance instead.

After your visa is approved

Book flights and temporary accommodation

Book a flexible or refundable flight if your visa is still pending. Arrange at least a few weeks of temporary accommodation — a dorm room or long-term rental rarely starts the day you land (see Accommodation).

Final week

Gather documents and pack

Carry originals and copies of every document you've collected — admission letter, visa, insurance proof, blocked account confirmation, academic transcripts, passport photos — in your hand luggage, not checked baggage.

03 — Interactive checklist

Track your progress

Common mistake

Students sometimes book a one-way flight before their visa is confirmed, or fund their blocked account below the required minimum. Both can delay your appointment by weeks. Double-check figures and dates directly with your embassy before transferring money.
