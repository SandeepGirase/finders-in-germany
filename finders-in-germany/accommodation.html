03 — Accommodation

Finding somewhere to live

Housing is the tightest market most students face in Germany, especially in university cities. Start searching the moment your admission is confirmed, and understand your three main options before you commit.

04 — Your options

Three ways to find a room

OptionWhat it isTrade-off
Studentenwohnheim (dorm)Subsidised student housing run by the local StudentenwerkCheapest option, but long waitlists — apply the day you're admitted
WG (Wohngemeinschaft)A shared flat with your own room and common kitchen/bathroomMost popular with students; quality depends heavily on flatmates
Private apartmentRenting a studio or flat on the open marketMost independence, but highest cost and most paperwork (proof of income, Schufa credit check)

Apply to the Studentenwerk immediately

Dorm waitlists in cities like Munich or Berlin can run six months or longer. Submit your dorm application the same week you accept your university offer, even if you plan to look for a WG as your main option.

05 — Where to search

Search platforms students actually use

For shared flats and rooms

WG-Gesucht and Studenten-WG are the two largest marketplaces for shared flats in Germany, with listings searchable by city and move-in date. Facebook groups for your specific university ("WG Zimmer [city]") are also active and often faster than formal listing sites.

For private rentals

ImmoScout24 and Immowelt list private apartments across Germany. Expect to compete with dozens of applicants for well-priced flats in major cities — a short, honest self-introduction (a Mieterselbstauskunft) helps you stand out.

Avoid rental scams

Never transfer a deposit before viewing the flat in person or by verified video call, and never pay a "landlord" who claims to be abroad and can only communicate by email. Genuine landlords in Germany almost always want to meet you or send an agent. If a price looks far below market rate for the area, treat it as a warning sign, not a lucky break.

Have an address?

You'll need it for your Anmeldung — the first registration every resident in Germany completes.
